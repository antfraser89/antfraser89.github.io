Privacy

Park! version 8 · last updated 11 November 2026

This page says what Park! does with information. The short version: the game keeps your progress on your own device and does not send it anywhere.

What the game stores

Your progress (parks, coins, spins, cards, pets, settings and the name and picture you choose for yourself) is saved in your browser's local storage on the device you play on. It is not sent to the publisher or to anyone else. Clearing your browser's site data, or choosing "Erase my data" in Settings, removes it for good.

A save code, if you make one, is that same progress written out as text. It goes wherever you paste it and nowhere else.

The four-digit support code in Settings is worked out from a random number in your save. It identifies a save, not a person.

What the game does not do

  • It has no accounts, and it does not ask for your name, email address, phone number, contacts or location.
  • It does not use analytics or tracking cookies.
  • The other players you see (in the club, the team build and the league table) are run by the game. Nothing you do is shown to other people.

Things loaded from elsewhere

The game's typefaces are loaded from Google Fonts. As with any web request, Google's servers see your device's internet address when they are fetched. Google's own privacy policy covers what it does with that.

After the first visit the game keeps a copy of itself on your device so it can be played without a connection.

Adverts and purchases

This version of the game shows no real adverts and takes no payments. If a later version connects an advert network, an app store or a payment provider, that provider will handle the advert or the payment under its own privacy policy, and this page will be updated to name it before that version is released.

Children

The game does not knowingly collect information from anyone, including children. It includes a slot-style machine played with pretend currency; see the Terms for more about that.

Changes and questions

If this page changes, the date at the top changes with it. Questions about privacy can be sent to the publisher through the page or store listing where you found the game.
